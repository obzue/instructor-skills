# Register Signal

Maximise signal. Calibrate the gain. Do not default every rewrite to polite or understated.

## Step 1 — Detect the channel

State the assumed channel in one line.

- Evaluation — resume, interview, bio, self-review
- Persuasion — post, listing story, article, pitch
- Technical peers — PR, firmware note, architecture, bug
- Stakeholder async — status, Slack, standup
- Business / vendor — supplier, client email, PO
- UK understatement — only when they ask to sound British or soften for a UK ear

Default to stakeholder async if unclear.

## Step 2 — Always-on habits

Apply in every channel except UK-understatement.

1. Claim first, context second. Cut throat-clearing.
2. State confidence. Remove stacked hedges that are not real uncertainty.
3. Keep impact numbers. Never delete a quantified result to sound modest. Never invent one.
4. Keep warmth, drop deference. No Sir, kindly, do the needful, humble request.

## Step 3 — Channel dials

- Evaluation — impact high, hedging off, own the result
- Persuasion — thesis sharp in sentence one
- Technical peers — direct on the claim, indirect only on the ask
- Stakeholder async — short, claim first, name the blocker
- Business / vendor — warm open, explicit ask, clear terms
- UK understatement — apply S.P.O.T. from references/spot.md. Never use T on resumes or interviews.

## Step 4 — Rewrite

Hold meaning fixed. Change structure, weight, and tone only.

Silent cleanup allowed. Articles, agreement, light-verb calques. Do not list the grammar fixes unless asked.

## Output

1. One line. Channel + dials that moved.
2. The rewritten text.

Add a change list only when the user asks what changed.

## Guardrails

- Preserve facts. Bracket missing numbers as `[need number]`.
- Do not flatten a long piece into one tone. Sharpen the spine.
- Keep the writer's voice.
- UK T-step is banned in evaluation contexts.
